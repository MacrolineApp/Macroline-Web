The research on tracking adherence keeps finding the same thing: the people who stick with it aren't more disciplined — their logging is just *cheaper*. Every tap you shave off the daily routine is compound interest on consistency.

So here's the speed tour. If logging breakfast takes you more than ten seconds, one of these will fix it.

## Favorites: the star is a shortcut

Star any food you log regularly and it pins to the top of your logging flow. The Greek yogurt you eat five days a week should never be a search result again — it should be sitting there waiting when the sheet opens. Two taps: the food, the log button.

## Saved meals: log the plate, not the parts

Your actual breakfast probably isn't one food — it's three. Two eggs, toast, the yogurt. Save that combination once as a meal ("weekday breakfast") and it logs as a unit forever after. This is the single biggest tap-saver in the app: four foods, portioned, logged, in the time it takes to pour the coffee.

Build them from your history, too — yesterday's well-logged dinner can become tonight's saved meal without re-entering anything.

## Real units: log the way you measured

"1.5 servings" is database-speak. Nobody eats 1.5 servings of rice; they eat a cup of it. Macroline lets you log in grams, ounces, cups — the unit your kitchen actually used — and converts against the food's real serving weight. Less mental math at the counter means less friction, and estimates that start closer to true.

## The pattern behind all three

You might notice a theme: none of these features are about *more data*. They're about lowering the cost of the log you were already going to make. We'd rather you log an honest breakfast in four seconds than abandon a perfect one in forty.

Set up three favorites and one saved meal tonight — tomorrow morning is your proof.
