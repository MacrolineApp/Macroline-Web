We spend a lot of words on this blog about accuracy — verified sources, provenance tiers, databases that correct themselves. So it might sound strange coming from us, but it needs saying:

**A mediocre log you keep beats a perfect log you quit.**

The math isn't close. Someone who logs "close enough" for a year learns more about their patterns than someone who logs flawlessly for eleven days and burns out. Trends live in weeks and months. No single Tuesday, however precisely measured, tells you anything a trend can't tell you better.

## Where tracking apps go wrong

Most trackers are built like compliance software. Miss a day and there's a broken streak waiting for you. Go over your goal and the numbers turn red. Log a birthday dinner and the app's whole demeanor says *incident report*.

That design philosophy quietly teaches one lesson: *if today won't be perfect, don't look at the app.* Which is exactly backwards. The days you least want to log are the ones carrying the most information.

We've made the opposite bets, and we'll keep making them:

- **No red numbers.** Going over a goal is information, not a violation.
- **No guilt copy.** The app doesn't editorialize about your choices. It counts.
- **Estimates are first-class.** An honest "roughly this" is real data, labeled as such. The alternative to a rough log isn't a precise log — it's no log.
- **Every day is a fresh page.** Yesterday's data informs; it doesn't indict.

## The practical version

If you take one habit from this post: **decouple logging from performing.** Log the day you'd rather forget, at whatever precision you can manage, and let it be boring. "Big restaurant dinner, estimated" is a complete, respectable diary entry. So is a 600-calorie appetite-crash day. So is a skipped afternoon reconstructed from memory the next morning.

The goal was never a beautiful diary. It's a long, honest one — because the value of your data compounds with its length, not its polish.

Accuracy matters. We've bet the company on it. But accuracy is a property of *data you actually have*. Open the app tomorrow. That's the whole strategy.
