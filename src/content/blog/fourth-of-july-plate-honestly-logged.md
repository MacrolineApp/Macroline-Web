Nobody is bringing a food scale to a barbecue. If your tracking system only works when you control the kitchen, it doesn't work — because Friday you'll be holding a paper plate of someone's uncle's ribs, and the app will be asking you for grams.

Here's the honest way through the Fourth.

## Estimates are allowed. That's the whole point of tiers.

Macroline labels every number with where it came from — and "estimated" is a *legitimate tier*, not a failure state. A cookout plate logged as a good-faith estimate is better data than a skipped day, and dramatically better than Monday-morning fiction.

The 90-second version, plate in hand:

- **Describe it.** "Cheeseburger with bun, scoop of potato salad, corn on the cob with butter, two beers." Let the AI break it into items. Eyeball the portions it guessed, nudge the obvious ones.
- **Round up the mystery items.** Uncle's ribs have a glaze? They're the sticky kind. Estimate generously — party food runs richer than home food, and an estimate that's honest about that beats one that flatters you.
- **Done.** Go back to the party. Seriously — logging is not a party trick, and standing in a corner weighing watermelon in your mind is not the brand.

## The math you should actually know

A big cookout day lands somewhere between 3,000 and 4,500 calories for most adults. That sounds dramatic. Across a week, it's a bump of maybe 15–20% on your weekly total — real, but recoverable by literally doing nothing except returning to normal.

Which brings up the actual risk of the Fourth. It was never the ribs.

## The day after is the whole game

The classic failure isn't the holiday — it's the three days *after*, when "I already blew it" turns one big meal into a lost week. The data says otherwise: one day is a blip. The blip only becomes a trend if you stop looking.

So the only rule that matters: **open the app on July 5th.** Log the leftover burger too. No penance macros, no punishment cardio math — just the next normal day, logged like any other.

Happy Fourth. Eat the ribs. Log the ribs. Move on.
